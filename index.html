We had no drones. No experience. Not even a team.
We signed up anyway.

One of us looked around a coworking space and asked four people if they were up for something none of us knew anything about. Everyone said yes. Why not.

Five software engineers. Five completely different backgrounds. Zero aerospace. No blueprint, no masterplan — just one question that wouldn’t let us go: how far can you actually get if you take AI seriously?

The arena: the MBDA & brigkAIR Swarm Drone Challenge 2026 — a serious international competition, backed by one of Europe’s largest defence technology groups. Not a university project. Not a hackathon. Teams from around the world, with real experience in autonomous systems.

Eight weeks. One drone. One target. We built an autonomous control system from scratch, competed against teams who’d been doing this for years — and made it through.

But that was just the beginning.

Because in the final, you don’t fly. You play.

Swarm versus swarm. Capture the Flag. Fully autonomous — once the mission starts, no human touches the controls. Six more weeks to turn a working system into one that holds up under real pressure, against real opponents, in a real arena.

The strategy? The AI decides. Live. On the field. It watches the opposing swarm, reads the situation, picks its tactics — and executes.

Top 6. International. ILA Berlin. Defence Track.
